John the Ripper can check 500,000 passwords per second on a recent PC with a supported graphics card. There's a good chance of recovery if your password:

1. is short -- ideally 8 or fewer characters
1. contains common strings, such as your name, "password", "qwerty", or "123456"
1. uses a consistent format, e.g. "abc" followed by four digits and a special character, or it's a combination of family names and birth dates.

If you used a random password generated by your browser or a password manager, check the settings in those applications -- it may be stored and synchronized across your devices.

If you have no idea about your password, [John the Ripper can try billions of combinations of characters](__/recover/incremental-run/) but processing could take considerable time.

Consider a six-character password that only uses the 52 upper and lower case western letters. This results in 52<sup>6</sup> combinations -- a total of almost 20 billion possibilities. Checking 500,000 passwords per second takes almost five hours to process them all.

A *typical* 6-character letter-only password could be recovered in three hours on a PC with a recent GPU. However, every additional character exponentially increases the number of password possibilities:

|password length|typical recovery time|
|-:|-|
|4 characters|7 seconds|
|5 characters|6 minutes|
|6 characters|3 hours|
|7 characters|12 days|
|8 characters|2 years|
|9 characters|90 years|
|10 characters|5,000 years|
|11 characters|240,000 years|
|12 characters|12 million years|

Using numbers and symbols increases the possibilities further, but having some idea about your password content or format can significantly reduce processing times.
