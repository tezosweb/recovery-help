John the Ripper can check 500,000 passwords per second on a recent PC with a supported graphics card. There's a good chance of recovery if your password:

1. is short -- ideally 8 or fewer characters.
1. contains common strings, such as your name, "password", "qwerty", or "123456".
1. uses a format you use regularly, e.g. "abc" followed by four digits and a special character, or it's a combination of family names and birth dates.

If you used a random password generated by your browser or a password manager, check the settings in those applications -- it may be stored and synchronized across your devices.

If you have no idea about your password, John the Ripper can try millions of combinations of characters but processing could take considerable time.

Consider a six-character password that only uses the 52 upper and lower case western letters. This results in 52<sup>6</sup> combinations -- a total of almost 20 billion possibilities. Checking 500,000 passwords per second takes almost five hours to process them all. It would take three hours to recover the *average* 6-character password.

Every character use exponentially increases the number of password possibilities:

* 7 characters: 12 days
* 8 characters: 2 years
* 9 characters: 90 years
* 10 characters: 5,000 years
* 11 characters: 240,000 years
* 12 characters: 12 million years

Using numbers and symbols increases the possibilities further, but having some idea about your password's format can reduce processing time to hours.
